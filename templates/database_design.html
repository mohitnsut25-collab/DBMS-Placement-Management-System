Academic DBMS Normalization Analysis (1NF, 2NF, 3NF)

Rigorous verification of First, Second, and Third Normal Forms, including Functional Dependencies and Lossless Join proofs.

1NF
First Normal Form

Rule: Each column contains atomic (indivisible) values; no repeating groups or composite lists.

Project Implementation: Student phones and HR contacts were decomposed from single comma/multivalued columns into dedicated child entities student_phone and company_contact_phone.
2NF
Second Normal Form

Rule: Must be in 1NF and all non-prime attributes must be fully functionally dependent on the whole candidate key (no partial dependencies).

Project Implementation: In composite key entities student_phone(student_id, phone_number) and company_contact_phone(contact_id, phone_number), phone_type depends on the combined key. In interview, evaluation attributes depend on {application_id, round_number}.
3NF
Third Normal Form

Rule: Must be in 2NF and contain no transitive dependencies ($X \to Y$, where $X$ is not a superkey and $Y$ is non-prime).

Project Implementation: Department info is separated from Student ($student\_id \to dept\_id \to dept\_name$), Company is separated from Job ($job\_id \to comp\_id \to comp\_name$), eliminating update anomalies.

Lossless-Join Decomposition & Dependency Preservation Proof

When decomposing relation $R$ into $R_1$ and $R_2$, the decomposition is lossless if and only if:
(R1 ∩ R2 → R1) ∨ (R1 ∩ R2 → R2)

  • Student → StudentPhone: $Student \cap StudentPhone = \{student\_id\}$, which is the primary key of $Student$. Hence, $student\_id \to Student$, proving the decomposition is strictly lossless.
  • Company → CompanyContact: $Company \cap CompanyContact = \{company\_id\}$, which is the primary key of $Company$. Natural join $Company \bowtie CompanyContact$ reconstructs original records without spurious tuples.
  • Dependency Preservation: Every functional dependency $X \to Y$ is directly enforceable within its decomposed relation or via relational foreign-key joins without querying other tables.

University DBMS Viva Examination Questions & Answers

Using phone1, phone2, phone3 violates First Normal Form (1NF) by introducing repeating groups, artificial limits on numbers, and excess NULL values when students have only one phone. Creating the normalized child entity student_phone allows an arbitrary count of phones per student, guarantees atomicity, and avoids schema alterations when business requirements change.

A student cannot register the exact same phone number multiple times. The combination of (student_id, phone_number) guarantees uniqueness without allocating a surrogate integer ID, accurately modelling a weak entity existence-dependent relationship.

In campus recruitment, the vast majority of applications do not progress to an offer. If offered_ctc, offer_date, and joining_date were placed inside application, the table would suffer from high sparsity (hundreds of NULL values for rejected candidates). Separating offer with UNIQUE(application_id) maintains clean 3NF and eliminates NULL anomalies.

RESTRICT blocks deletion of a parent record if dependent child rows exist (e.g. deleting a student with active applications is blocked to protect historical audit data). CASCADE automatically propagates deletions down to child rows (e.g. deleting a company removes its HR contacts and contact phone lines to prevent orphan records).

One student can apply to multiple jobs, and one job can receive applications from multiple students. application acts as an associative entity (bridge table) with foreign keys referencing Student(student_id) and Job(job_id), supplemented by UNIQUE(student_id, job_id) to ensure a candidate cannot apply to the same position twice.

Every relation satisfies 1NF (all attributes are atomic) and 2NF (no partial functional dependencies on any candidate key). Furthermore, all transitive dependencies have been decomposed ($X \to Y$ holds only when $X$ is a superkey), eliminating redundancy and insertion/deletion anomalies across all 10 entities.
